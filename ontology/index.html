Ontologies, explained by building one.

An ontology is a shared, machine-readable model of a domain: the kinds of things that exist, how they relate, and the rules that hold. Give it to a reasoner and it derives facts nobody wrote down.

New to this? The 30-second version

Imagine briefing a very literal new colleague: "There are animals. Dogs are a kind of animal. Rex is a dog." Now ask them "Is Rex an animal?" They can work it out, even though you never said it directly.

An ontology is that briefing, written so a computer can read it. A reasoner is the colleague who works out the answers. No maths or code needed here: you'll build one by clicking.

People use the terms loosely: some call the ontology plus its data "the knowledge graph", and many graph databases run with little formal ontology. In Build a World, your classes and rules are the ontology; your individuals and facts are a small knowledge graph.

Jargon decoder

The words you'll see, in plain English.

Ontology
A description of the kinds of things in a subject, how they relate, and the rules they follow, written so a computer can use it.
Reasoner
The program that reads your rules and works out new facts.
Subclass
A narrower kind. Every Dog is an Animal, so Dog is a subclass of Animal.
Domain
The type given to whatever starts a relationship. If the domain of "owns" is Person, anyone who owns something is a Person.
Range
The type given to whatever a relationship points to. If the range of "owns" is Vehicle, anything owned is a Vehicle.
Transitive
A relationship that chains: inside a box inside a room means inside the room.
Inverse
The same link read backwards: "parent of" and "child of".
Disjoint
Two classes with no members in common. Nothing is both a Cat and a Dog.
OWL & Turtle
OWL is the W3C standard for ontologies. Turtle is a readable text format for writing it.
Inference
A fact the reasoner works out from your rules, rather than one you typed in. Shown as dashed amber arrows.
Contradiction
Facts that can't all be true under your rules, such as something being in two disjoint classes. Usually a sign of bad data.
Open world
Anything not stated or inferred is unknown, not false. Databases usually assume the opposite: no row means no.
Knowledge graph
Real things and the links between them, stored as a graph and usually organised by an ontology.
RDF & SPARQL
RDF is the W3C data model behind OWL and most knowledge graphs: every fact is a subject, a predicate, and an object. SPARQL queries it, much as SQL queries tables.
R2RML
A W3C standard for mapping database tables to an ontology, so the data can be queried as a knowledge graph.
Semantic layer
Shared business definitions, such as what "revenue" means, sitting between the warehouse and BI or AI tools.
